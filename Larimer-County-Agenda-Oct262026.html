Our point of view

If the platform is set up properly, the guardrails are documented, and people are trained, departments can build most of their own AI tools. IT governs the platform instead of fielding every request, and the county doesn't depend on a large integrator to make progress.

How we work

A fractional AI team that scales to your backlog

What we provide

  • Business analysis: finding and prioritizing the use cases worth doing.
  • Infrastructure and security: configured in your Google Cloud and Workspace tenant, under least-privilege access, to your standards.
  • Pilots: built alongside your staff, so your team learns by doing.
  • Training: train-the-trainer, office hours, prompt libraries, and runbooks you keep.

How the work runs

  • The county owns the backlog and sets priorities monthly.
  • A capped weekly team works the list, so spend is always known.
  • Agents answer from your own documents, with citations. Questions they can't answer go to a person or a ticket.
  • Every agent comes with a dashboard showing what's resolved and what isn't.

Two lanes for building

  • No-code agents in Gemini Enterprise for knowledge and policy questions. Business staff can maintain these.
  • Integrations such as ticketing or HR systems, built with your application development team, who own them afterward.

Governance from day one

  • County data stays in the county tenant.
  • Standards for who can build agents and how they're reviewed before wider release.
  • Start with informational use cases, with people making decisions that affect individuals.
  • Work aligned with Colorado requirements, including open records and the state AI law effective January 1, 2027.

  • Use case deep dive: a working session to identify and prioritize the use cases with the most value for county staff.
  • Scoping and capability review: a closer look at what's realistic with the current Google Cloud and Workspace environment, and what each option would take.
  • Current state of AI application development: a review with the application development team of what's been built and tested, and where it could go next.
